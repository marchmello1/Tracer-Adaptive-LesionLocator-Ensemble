\begin{abstract}
Interactive whole-body PET/CT segmentation must preserve a strong automatic
mask while translating sparse corrections into changes of the intended lesion
instances. We present a stateless system for FDG and PSMA studies that combines
a five-fold residual-encoder U-Net ensemble, tracer-specific calibration, and
a prompt-conditioned distance-transform model. The central contribution is a
component-wise topology ledger. Candidate additions are accepted only when
they do not merge accepted lesions, and supervised removals are accepted only
when they do not fragment them. Input-grid annotations are mapped to cropped
model space with one explicit origin subtraction, and cumulative annotations
are replayed from the same image-only prediction at every call. On nine defined
local interaction trajectories, the final policy increased mean AUC-Dice from
0.8403 to 0.8443 and mean AUC-DMM/F1 from 0.9136 to 0.9139, with no observed
case-level regression. A five-case preliminary evaluation reported Dice
0.8546, lesion F1 0.8342, and mean metric position 3.5. Container-level checks
on one FDG and one PSMA fixture produced byte-identical outputs before and
after runtime modularization. These limited experiments establish behavioral
reproducibility, but do not determine generalization to the hidden final set.
\keywords{autoPET V \and interactive segmentation \and PET/CT \and topology}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Whole-body PET/CT combines metabolic and anatomical information, but lesion
appearance and physiological uptake differ substantially between FDG and PSMA
tracers. Automatic segmentation therefore faces both voxel-level uncertainty
and an instance-level problem: a mask may obtain high overlap while missing a
small lesion, retaining a physiological false positive, or joining separate
tumors. Interactive evaluation adds a further requirement. A useful correction
should modify the lesion indicated by a sparse annotation without destabilizing
unrelated parts of the volume.

AutoPET V evaluates this setting through cumulative simulated and
clinician-driven annotations. Our starting point is the multitracer
LesionTracer ensemble~\cite{rokuss2024multitracer} and the promptable
LesionLocator model~\cite{rokuss2025interactive}, both based on nnU-Net
v2~\cite{isensee2021nnunet}. Rather than retraining either network, we study the
interface between their predictions. The research question is whether simple,
explicit component invariants can make prompt fusion safer while retaining the
accuracy of the image-only ensemble.

The contributions are threefold. First, we define a stateless tracer-aware
pipeline that reconstructs the initial mask and replays all cumulative
annotations. Second, we introduce component-wise acceptance tests that prevent
one useful proposal from concealing an unrelated merge or split. Third, we
publish a modular runtime with unit, policy-equivalence, and container-level
reproduction evidence. All thresholds and pretrained checkpoints are frozen
for the submitted system.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data}

\subsubsection{Training data}
No model was trained for this submission. The image-only checkpoints were
trained on the public AutoPET-III data by their publishers, following the
multitracer training procedure in~\cite{rokuss2024multitracer}. The
interactive checkpoint was trained on the public AutoPET-IV data with
synthetic click generation as described in~\cite{rokuss2025interactive}.
These collections derive from the public FDG and PSMA PET/CT resources
described in~\cite{gatidis2022fdg,jeblick2024psma}. We introduce no additional
training subjects, labels, or weight optimization.

\subsubsection{Validation data}
Engineering validation used ten local challenge-format fixtures: six FDG and
four PSMA studies, each replayed for up to five cumulative interaction steps.
One FDG fixture had no defined Dice or lesion-F1 value because both the target
and prediction were effectively negative for the evaluated quantity. We
therefore report paired aggregate metrics over the remaining nine defined
trajectories. These fixtures were repeatedly used during policy development
and must not be interpreted as an independent estimate of hidden-test
performance.

\subsection{System overview}

Let $I=(I_{CT},I_{PET})$ denote a co-registered volume and let
$P_t=(P_t^+,P_t^-)$ contain all foreground and background annotation voxels
available at interaction step $t$. The system computes
\begin{equation}
K_0 = C_{\tau}(E(I)), \qquad
K_t = F(K_0,D(I,P_t),P_t;\pi_{\tau}),
\end{equation}
where $E$ is the five-fold image-only ensemble, $C_{\tau}$ is
tracer-conditioned calibration, $D$ is the prompt-conditioned donor model,
$F$ is component-wise reconciliation, and $\pi_{\tau}$ is the fixed FDG or
PSMA interaction policy. Each call reconstructs $K_0$ and evaluates the full
cumulative $P_t$; no mask from step $t-1$ is required. This makes identical
requests deterministic with respect to saved inputs and checkpoints.

\subsection{Preprocessing and coordinate mapping}

CT and PET volumes are processed with the normalization, target spacing, and
sliding-window configuration stored in the pinned nnU-Net plans. Predictions
are returned to the CT reference grid and copy its size, spacing, origin, and
direction. An annotation coordinate on the input grid is converted to cropped
model space by subtracting the crop origin exactly once and then applying the
crop-to-resampled scale. Coordinates with malformed dimensionality or outside
the volume are rejected before model execution.

\subsection{Tracer routing and initial segmentation}

A frozen three-classifier vote assigns each study to the FDG or PSMA policy.
The image-only mask is generated by five 3-D residual-encoder U-Net folds with
mirror test-time augmentation disabled. FDG foreground probabilities are
thresholded at 0.47. Components smaller than 25 voxels are removed, except
when at least 25 robust components indicate high disease burden, in which case
the dust threshold is relaxed to five voxels. PSMA uses threshold 0.50 and a
five-voxel dust threshold. A frozen logistic component classifier then rejects
PSMA candidates above a false-positive probability of 0.86. The classifier
uses fixed component-level image and geometry features; it is not adapted on
the evaluation fixtures.

\subsection{Prompt-conditioned donor}

When effective annotations are present, a fold-0 EDT LesionLocator model
receives CT, PET, and all cumulative prompts and produces a binary donor mask
$D_t$. Tumor propagation is active for every annotated FDG call. For PSMA it
is activated after at least six cumulative foreground points and only when
$K_0$ contains at most 128 connected components. This evidence threshold
limits changes from very sparse prompts, while the component ceiling avoids
applying the donor to extremely fragmented initial masks.

\subsection{Topology ledger}

All masks use 18-connectivity. Let $n(M)$ be the number of foreground
connected components in mask $M$. The ledger starts from $M=K_0$ and is the
only module permitted to mutate the accepted mask. For each donor component
$A$ selected by a foreground annotation, addition is accepted iff
\begin{equation}
n(M\cup A) \ge n(M).
\label{eq:add}
\end{equation}
Because proposals are evaluated one connected component at a time,
Eq.~\ref{eq:add} rejects a proposal that bridges two accepted lesions instead
of allowing a separate newly discovered component to compensate for that
merge. On acceptance, $M\leftarrow M\cup A$ and the component count is
recomputed.

Foreground annotation strokes are also direct supervision. Each connected
stroke is added without dilation and is subjected to the same non-merging
test. Broad background component replacement is disabled in the submitted
runtime. For PSMA, an explicitly annotated background stroke $R$ is removed
only iff
\begin{equation}
n(M\setminus R) \le n(M),
\label{eq:remove}
\end{equation}
which prevents a thin erasure from splitting one accepted lesion into several
objects. FDG background strokes do not mutate the submitted mask. These rules
preserve component-count direction, not full digital topology; this distinction
is considered in the limitations.

\subsection{Postprocessing and deployment}

The reconciled binary mask is transposed back to the challenge array order,
resampled to the reference geometry, and written as one tumor-lesion
segmentation. The Grand Challenge container performs no network access during
inference and reads the model archive from a read-only mount. The submitted
image contains five initial folds, one interactive fold interface, the frozen
tracer router, and the component-filter parameters described above.

\subsection{Training and test parameters}

Both segmentation networks use the pinned 3-D full-resolution nnU-Net plans:
$192\times192\times192$ voxel patches, source batch size two, and a
residual-encoder U-Net architecture. The upstream trainers use compound soft
Dice and cross-entropy supervision. No learning rate, optimizer, scheduler, or
augmentation was introduced because weights were not updated. Inference uses
five-fold ensembling for $K_0$, one EDT fold for interactive calls, and no
mirror TTA. Engineering and validation used up to six NVIDIA H100 80~GB HBM3
GPUs. Deployment targets an NVIDIA T4 with 16~GB VRAM; the highest sampled
allocation in the two container fixtures was approximately 7.1~GB.

\subsection{Implementation and availability}

Challenge I/O, initial prediction, donor execution, and reconciliation are
separate modules. Prompt validation and tracer policy are immutable data
objects, while the topology ledger owns mask changes. The Apache-2.0 source,
tests, build recipes, notices, checkpoint hashes, and preprint are available at
\texttt{github.com/marchmello1/Tracer-Adaptive-LesionLocator-Ensemble}.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Paired causal replay}

The v2 policy and final v3 policy were compared on identical images and
cumulative annotations. At each step the two policies started from the same
$K_0$ and received the same donor output; thus the comparison isolates the
exact foreground-stroke rule. Dice was computed as
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Dice}(M,G)=\frac{2|M\cap G|}{|M|+|G|}.
\end{equation}
Lesion detection used the challenge DMM/F1 evaluator. We summarize each
five-step trajectory by its causal area under the metric curve and then take
the unweighted mean over the nine defined cases. No confidence interval is
reported because the fixtures are a small, development-selected cohort.

\subsection{Packaging reproduction}

The modular policy was compared with the frozen policy in 1,000 randomized
mask/prompt trials. We then ran the frozen and modular containers with
identical model mounts, environment, and inputs on one FDG and one PSMA
fixture. Finally, the runtime was embedded into a self-contained Docker image
and rerun without a host code mount. Output files were compared by SHA-256,
not merely by aggregate metrics. The repository contains 45 focused tests for
I/O validation, policy activation, component filtering, topology guards,
idempotence, and release layout.

obtained Dice 0.854649, lesion F1/DMM 0.834179, and mean metric position 3.5.

The modular v3 image was not separately submitted to the preliminary set.

All 1,000 randomized production-policy comparisons agreed. The FDG and PSMA
container fixtures were byte-identical between the frozen and modular
runtimes, and the self-contained PSMA rerun retained the same output hash.
These results establish equivalence for the tested conditions, not a formal
proof over all image geometries or GPU execution paths.

\section{Discussion}

The results support a narrow conclusion: explicit component-level guards can
improve correction behavior without changing network weights or degrading the
observed development trajectories. The per-component evaluation is important.
A batch union may preserve its total component count when one proposal creates
a lesion while another joins two existing lesions; processing components
individually prevents that cancellation. Exact foreground strokes provide a
second, model-independent path for honoring supervision, while the
non-merging guard limits their spatial effect.

The tracer policies reflect different failure modes. FDG preserves a strong
automatic mask and accepts prompt-selected donor components immediately. PSMA
uses stricter evidence and a frozen false-positive filter because physiological
uptake and high lesion burden create more ambiguous components. These choices
are transparent and testable, although fixed thresholds may not transfer to a
different scanner or cohort distribution.

\section{Limitations}

The validation cohort is small, repeatedly inspected, and unsuitable for
estimating final-set rank. The preliminary set contains only five studies and
is likewise an implementation check. The component-count inequalities do not
preserve holes, handles, or anatomical identity; they are conservative
surrogates for two observed instance errors. The six-point PSMA threshold,
128-component ceiling, and probability thresholds were not estimated with an
independent calibration cohort. Only one FDG and one PSMA fixture were used for
container-level equivalence, and sampled memory is not a worst-case bound.
Finally, frozen public checkpoints inherit the data composition, annotation
quality, and subgroup biases of their original training sets. The system is a
research prototype and is not intended for clinical decision-making.

\section{Conclusion}

Tracer Adaptive LesionLocator Ensemble combines a multitracer image-only
ensemble with prompt-conditioned proposals through a stateless topology
ledger. Component-wise non-merging and non-fragmenting checks make interactive
changes explicit, local, and reproducible. Local paired replay suggests a
small improvement without observed regression, while container tests confirm
behavioral equivalence after modularization. Larger independent evaluation is
required to establish generalization and clinical utility.
